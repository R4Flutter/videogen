# LONGFORM VOX — the 10-minute essay

`vox_style_engine.md` built the vocabulary. It was built for the 60–90 second
lane and it is good at it: 15 modules, kinetic type on real word timings,
roughjs marks, paper that drifts. This document is the plan for the other
lane — **a 10-minute, 16:9, studio-grade scam explainer**, rendered by the same
`script.json` → `VoxShort.tsx` pipeline, with no paid service anywhere in it.

The engine does not need to be rewritten. It needs five things it doesn't have,
and one of them isn't code.

---

## PART 0 — THE HONEST VERDICT

| | You have | 10 minutes needs |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | 15 modules, all shipping | ✅ enough — repetition is the risk, not coverage |
| Staging | `VoxShort.tsx` sizes off canvas, `VoxEssay` already 1920×1080 | ✅ works today, untouched |
| Timing | `align.py` — voice is the clock | ✅ correct architecture, needs caching |
| Structure | flat list of beats | ❌ **no acts, no chapter cards, no re-hooks** |
| Voice | Chatterbox, one take per beat, built-in speaker | ❌ **drifts across 70 takes** |
| Images | prompt sheet → you generate → `footage.json` | ⚠️ works, but 80 slots by hand needs a gate |
| Rhythm | parser picks a module per beat, greedily | ❌ **nothing stops `kinetic` seven times** |
| Render | one pass, 1,013 frames | ❌ **18,000 frames, one crash = start over** |

Four of those five are a weekend of work. The fifth — structure — is a writing
discipline, and it is the one that decides whether anybody watches to 10:00.

**The engine will happily render slop for ten minutes.** Everything in Part 4
exists because of that sentence.

## PART 1 — THE ARITHMETIC

Run these numbers before writing a script. They are the whole production plan.

```
runtime            600 s @ 30 fps  =  18,000 frames, 1920×1080
narration          ~1,300 words    (Vox reads 145–155 wpm, ~85% speech density)
beats              70–80           (average beat 7.5–8.5 s)
acts               5 + cold open + close
image slots        22–26 photo beats × 3 framings  =  70–80 images
prompt sheets      7–8 files of 10  (video/prompts/01.md … 08.md)
TTS takes          ~180 sentence chunks (see Part 2.2), not 75
whisper alignment  ~10 min of audio
render             18,000 frames ≈ 45–90 min on 8–16 cores, chunked
```

Two numbers matter more than the rest:

- **1,300 words.** That is 4–6 genuine *mechanisms*. Not six restatements of one
  mechanism. If your outline has three, you have a 6-minute video — cut it to 6
  minutes rather than padding, because padding is visible and the retention
  graph shows exactly where you started padding.
- **80 images, generated by hand.** At ~40 s each that's an hour of clicking,
  once per episode. Tolerable — but only if nothing else in the pipeline makes
  you do it twice. Hence the caching and the missing-slot gate below.

## PART 2 — THE FIVE BLOCKERS

### 2.1 Structure — the parser has no idea what an act is

`parse-script.mjs` reads `### BEAT n` blocks into a flat array. At 6 beats that's
correct. At 75 beats a flat array is a slideshow: no breath, no chapter, no
place for the music to change, no re-hook, and nothing on screen that tells a
viewer at 4:30 where they are in the argument.

**Fix — acts as a first-class thing.**

1. Parse `## ACT 2 — HOW THE PAYOUT WORKS (2:10–4:05)` headings. Every beat
   underneath carries `act: 2` and `actName` into `script.json`.
2. Auto-insert a `chapter` beat at each act boundary — a 2.5-second card, the
   act title in Archivo 800 on paper, accent rule drawing under it, no
   narration. This is the breath. The crime engine already has this component
   (`crime/scenes.tsx:152`, "a breath between developments, not an act break") —
   port the idea, not the file.
3. `**Act Hook:**` row on the act heading: one sentence of narration that plays
   *over* the card. This is the re-hook, and it is the single biggest retention
   lever in a 10-minute video. Every act opens with a question the viewer now
   wants answered.

Cost: ~60 lines in `parse-script.mjs`, one new `src/vox/chapter.tsx`, one line
in `VOX_MODULES`.

### 2.2 Voice — 75 independent Chatterbox takes do not sound like one narrator

This is the thing that will make a 10-minute video feel amateur no matter how
good the graphics are. Chatterbox with no reference voice re-invents the
speaker on every call. Across 6 beats you don't notice. Across 75 you hear a
different person every 30 seconds, and `voice.py`'s own comment already admits
the pace dial barely moves: *"the actual pace lever is a reference voice."*

**Four fixes, in order of impact:**

1. **Pin one reference voice. Do this first, before anything else in this doc.**
   Record or select a single clean 15–20 s sample, save it as
   `brand/narrator.wav`, commit it, and make `--voice brand/narrator.wav` the
   default in `voice.py` rather than a flag. Every take in every episode then
   inherits one timbre and one tempo. This costs 20 minutes and fixes the
   biggest quality gap in the stack.

2. **Chunk at the sentence, not the beat.** Chatterbox degrades past ~300
   characters — and a long-form beat is 3–4 sentences. Split each beat's `Audio`
   on sentence boundaries, synthesise each, concatenate with the module's
   `DELIVERY` silence between them. Two wins: the read stops falling apart on
   long beats, and `align.py` gets short windows, which measurably improves word
   timings — which is what `KineticText` runs on.

3. **Cache by content hash.** Hash `(text, exaggeration, cfg, ref-voice)` into a
   `beat-N.json` sidecar. Skip any chunk whose hash is unchanged. Without this,
   fixing one typo at 7:20 re-runs the entire episode's TTS. With it, a script
   edit costs seconds.

4. **Normalise and gate.**
   - `ffmpeg -af loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11` per take. Level jumps between
     beats read as "AI video" faster than anything else on this list.
   - A high-pass at 80 Hz, and that's all the processing you need.
   - **Retake gate:** `align.py` already transcribes every take with
     faster-whisper. Diff that transcript against the scripted words — it
     already computes this alignment — and fail loudly on any chunk over ~12%
     word error. A mumbled or dropped line at 6:40 is not something you want to
     find after an 80-minute render.

### 2.3 Images — 80 slots, generated by hand, must never be generated twice

The prompt-sheet workflow is right and should not change. `scene-prompts.mjs`
already builds every prompt from the beat's own rows, strips storyboard
direction, appends the house style, prints the negative separately, reserves the
typography zone, and asks for isolated subjects on clean negative space. That
last detail is worth more than it looks — Part 3.5 spends it.

At 80 slots it needs three additions:

1. **Set `**Format:** landscape` in the script header.** `scene-prompts.mjs`
   reads `script.width/height` for both `ASPECT` and `RESERVED`. Leave the
   header on portrait and render `VoxEssay` anyway and you will generate 80
   vertical images with the wrong safe zones. Cheapest possible mistake to make
   and the most expensive to fix.
2. **Sheets per act, not per ten.** `prompts/act-2.md` is a session you can
   finish; `prompts/04.md` is a file with no meaning. Keep 10 per file inside
   each act.
3. **A missing-slot gate.** `npm run footage -- --report` prints every slot with
   no file and exits non-zero. Wire it into `vox:build` ahead of `render`.
   Rendering 18,000 frames and discovering beat 47 staged as a bare paper page
   is the failure mode this prevents.

Free sources that stay free, in the order you should reach for them:

| Need | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The 80 house-style stills | your own generator | prompt sheet is already built for it |
| Objects, places, documents, machines | Wikimedia Commons (`--commons`) | already wired, 3000–5000 px, attribution auto-written to `credits.json` |
| App screens, chat threads, transfers | `chat-mockup.py`, `transfer-mockup.py` | draw the interface — never hunt for a photo of a UI |
| Motion b-roll | Pexels / Pixabay video | free, no attribution required; use sparingly, a real clip breaks the paper look if it's graded wrong |

### 2.4 Rhythm — nothing prevents `kinetic` seven times in a row

The parser picks a module per beat from the `Visual` line, greedily, with no
memory. Across 6 beats the author holds the variety in their head. Across 75
they will not, and the failure is invisible in the script and obvious in the
video.

**Fix — `tools/rundown.mjs`, a linter that prints the episode as a rundown and
fails the build on the rules below.** Add it to `npm run check`.

```
RUNDOWN — THE TWO-DOLLAR TRAP — 9:58 — 74 beats — 5 acts

ACT 1  THE OFFER                                    0:42–2:38
  7  kinetic   0:42  8.1s  PAID TO WATCH VIDEOS
  8  doodle    0:50  7.4s  the job is real          [img 3/3]
  9  icon      0:58  9.2s  three trust signals
 10  stat      1:07  6.1s  $2
 ...
  photo load 31%  ·  data modules 4  ·  longest hold 11.2s  ✅

ACT 3  WHERE THE MONEY GOES                         4:05–6:20
 ...
  photo load 52%  ⚠  data modules 1  ⚠  kinetic ×3 in 5 beats  ❌
```

The rules it enforces — these are the difference between a Vox video and a deck:

- **No module twice in a row.** Ever. `kinetic` → `kinetic` is the tell.
- **No module more than twice in any 6-beat window** (`kinetic` may reach 3).
- **A data module every 90 seconds.** `chart` `compare` `stat` `timeline`
  `trace` `funnel`. This is the format's actual claim to authority; a stretch of
  ninety seconds with no quantity on screen is a podcast with pictures.
- **Photo-bearing modules ≤ 35% of runtime.** Past that it stops being an
  editorial page and becomes a stock slideshow with captions. The paper is the
  brand; the photographs are guests on it.
- **No beat over 12 seconds.** A single frame held for 12 s dies regardless of
  what's on it. Split the beat; the narration doesn't change.
- **Every act opens on `chapter`, closes on `kinetic` or `stat`.** Acts land.
- **`quote` at least twice per episode.** `vox_style_engine.md` house rule 5:
  cite what you didn't compute. In the scam niche this is also your defence.

### 2.5 Render — 18,000 frames, one pass, no restart

Your last render encoded 1,013 frames. Ten minutes is 18× that, and a single
pass means a crash at frame 14,000 costs the whole session.

**Fix — `tools/render-chunks.mjs`:**

```
for each 1,800-frame chunk (60 s):
    if out/chunks/NN.mp4 exists and its frame range is unchanged -> skip
    remotion render VoxEssay out/chunks/NN.mp4 --frames=A-B --concurrency=C
ffmpeg -f concat -safe 0 -i list.txt -c copy out/episode.mp4
```

Restartable, resumable, and re-rendering one fixed act costs 4 minutes instead
of 80. Notes for your machine (8–16 cores):

- `--concurrency` = **cores ÷ 2**. Each worker is a headless Chrome; oversubscribing
  makes it slower, not faster, and can OOM at 1080p.
- `Config.setVideoImageFormat("jpeg")` is already set. Keep it.
- **Proxy pass for review:** `--scale=0.5 --every-nth-frame=2` → roughly 8×
  faster. Watch the proxy, fix, then run the real one once. Never review a
  10-minute video by rendering it at full res.
- Concat requires identical encoder settings across chunks — set them explicitly
  in `render-chunks.mjs` rather than relying on defaults matching.
- Audio: render the VO+music bed once, mux it onto the concatenated video at the
  end. Do not let each chunk encode its own audio tail.

Expect **45–90 minutes** for a finished 10-minute 1080p30 episode.

## PART 3 — THE PHASES

Each phase ends with something renderable. Do not batch them.

### Phase 1 — the reference voice (½ day)
`brand/narrator.wav` + `voice.py` defaults to it. Re-render the existing 34 s
short and listen. **Ship nothing else until this sounds like one person.**

### Phase 2 — acts and chapter cards (1 day)
`parse-script.mjs` act parsing → `src/vox/chapter.tsx` → registered in
`VOX_MODULES`. Write a 3-minute, 3-act test script to prove the structure before
committing to 10.

### Phase 3 — voice at scale (1 day)
Sentence chunking, content-hash caching, loudnorm, the WER retake gate in
`align.py`.

### Phase 4 — the rundown linter (½ day)
`tools/rundown.mjs`, wired into `npm run check`. Run it against the 3-minute
test and fix what it complains about — the rules are only useful if you obey
them when they're inconvenient.

### Phase 5 — chunked render (½ day)
`tools/render-chunks.mjs`, `npm run render:long`, `npm run preview:long`.

### Phase 6 — the long-form finish pass (1–2 days)
The things that separate "correct" from "studio":

- **Act-aware music.** One bed for 10 minutes is death by monotony. Four beds —
  `bed-act1.wav` … — cross-faded on chapter cards, with the existing `riser.wav`
  landing on the card itself. `Soundtrack`'s duck logic is already right; extend
  `bed(t)` to be act-aware instead of a single interpolate.
- **A source bar.** A lower-third that prints `Source:` for four seconds under
  any beat carrying a `**Source:**` row. Free credibility, and in the fraud
  niche it is also what keeps the video up.
- **Parallax on stills.** Your prompt already demands *"every element isolated
  against clean negative space, on its own plane, so a motion designer can cut
  it out and move it independently."* Spend that: run `cutout.py` on the hero,
  put subject and paper ground on two layers drifting at different rates. A
  still that moves in two planes holds 8 seconds; a flat still held 8 seconds is
  where viewers leave.
- **A cold-open callback.** The closing beat restates the opening number. It's a
  20-word script change and it is why people comment.

## PART 4 — THE 10-MINUTE SCRIPT SPEC

Same beat block as `VOX_MODULES.md`. Two new things: `## ACT` headings and the
`**Act Hook:**` row.

```markdown
# THE TWO-DOLLAR TRAP

**Style:** Vox deep dive
**Format:** landscape
**Runtime:** 10:00
**Caption Hook:** They pay $2 for videos. Then ask $250. The trap is the trust.
**Keywords:** task scam, fake job, telegram scam, money laundering

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:42)

### BEAT 1 — THE HOOK (0:00–0:06)

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **On-screen text** | PAID TO WATCH VIDEOS |
| **Visual** | Words fill the page as they're spoken. |
| **Audio** | They pay you two dollars to watch videos. Then they ask for two hundred fifty. |

## ACT 2 — HOW THE PAYOUT WORKS (2:10–4:05)

**Act Hook:** The money arrives. That's what makes the next part work.

### BEAT 18 — THE FIRST PAYOUT (2:14–2:23)
...
```

**Act shape for 10 minutes** — five acts, one mechanism each, each 90–140 s:

| | Runs | Job | Opens on | Lands on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–0:42 | the number + the stake. No title, no greeting, no "in this video". | `kinetic` | `stat` |
| **Act 1 — The Offer** | 0:42–2:10 | how it presents itself, and why that's plausible | `chapter` | `doodle` |
| **Act 2 — The Payout** | 2:10–4:05 | mechanism one: why the first money is real | `chapter` | `trust` |
| **Act 3 — The Ask** | 4:05–6:20 | mechanism two: the turn, and why people say yes | `chapter` | `compare` |
| **Act 4 — The Money** | 6:20–8:10 | mechanism three: where it actually goes | `chapter` | `trace` / `map` |
| **Act 5 — The Tell** | 8:10–9:20 | what breaks it — what a viewer does differently | `chapter` | `icon` |
| **Close** | 9:20–10:00 | the callback. The opening number, re-read. | `kinetic` | *no fade* |

`vox_style_engine.md` PART 5: **the final beat doesn't fade.** Still true at ten
minutes, and more important — the payoff is the line the whole essay was built
to land.

## PART 5 — WHY ANYONE STAYS FOR TEN MINUTES

The short rules in `vox_style_engine.md` PART 1 still hold — hook under 15
words, start mid-action, a number beats a claim, no meta-commentary, no hype
adjectives, no fake scenarios. These are the ones that only exist at length:

1. **One open loop, stated in the cold open, closed in the last 40 seconds.**
   Name the thing you will not explain yet. "Two hundred and fifty dollars left
   this account in eleven minutes. Here's where it went." Everything between is
   the viewer waiting for that.
2. **A question at every act card.** The card is not a title, it is a promise.
   "Why did the first payout clear?" earns the next 90 seconds.
3. **But / Therefore, never And / And.** Every beat must be the consequence of
   or the contradiction of the one before. Three consecutive beats joined by
   "and" is where the retention graph turns down.
4. **The mechanism is the content.** House rule 6. At ten minutes you need four
   of them, each one a thing the viewer could not have guessed and can repeat to
   someone else afterwards.
5. **Escalate the stakes across acts, not within them.** Act 4's number is
   bigger than Act 2's. If it isn't, reorder the acts.
6. **A viewer-facing payoff in Act 5.** The scam niche's compounding advantage:
   the person watching wants to not be next. Give them the one tell that
   generalises, and they send the video to their parents. That is the entire
   growth strategy for this channel.
7. **Cite it.** A cite costs four seconds and is the difference between an
   explainer and a guy asserting numbers. In fraud, it is also the difference
   between a video and a claim about a named party.

## PART 7 — THE GATE BEFORE YOU PUBLISH

Watch the proxy once, top to bottom, with this list. If any line fails, it's a
re-render, not a "next time".

- [ ] First 5 seconds contain a number and no greeting
- [ ] Every act card asks a question
- [ ] No module twice in a row anywhere (`npm run rundown` says so)
- [ ] A quantity on screen at least every 90 seconds
- [ ] Photo-bearing beats under 35% of runtime
- [ ] No beat holds a still frame longer than 12 seconds
- [ ] Every stated figure you didn't compute has a `Source` row
- [ ] Levels: no audible jump between any two beats
- [ ] The narrator sounds like the same person at 0:30 and at 9:30
- [ ] Accent orange points at something in every frame it appears in — never decorates
- [ ] One mark per frame
- [ ] The last beat does not fade
- [ ] `credits.json` is complete — Commons is CC-BY, it's a licence obligation
