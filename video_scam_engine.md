# VIDEO SCAM ENGINE — The Pivot Plan

Your crime documentary engine is solid. We're keeping 70% of the bones and
pivoting the niche. This document is the full blueprint: why the pivot wins,
what changes in the engine, what stays, and the exact 14-day path to your
first scam-explainer viral short.

---

## PART 0 — WHY SCAMS, NOT CRIME

### The case for the pivot

Crime doc was a smart first build. It taught you the engine. But the niche
itself has 3 problems you can't fix with better production:

1. **Image scarcity.** Real crime scenes, suspects, evidence — you can't show
   any of it. You end up with text, moody B-roll, and 90 seconds of "police
   station hallway" footage on repeat. That's why the doc felt boring.

2. **Legal/policy ceiling.** YouTube auto-flags crime content more aggressively
   in 2026. The category is also under monetization pressure from advertisers
   (financial brands pull out of "graphic violence" adjacency buckets). CPM
   drops 30–50% vs equivalent-length educational content.

3. **One-watch content.** True crime has a "solved mystery" ceiling — once the
   viewer knows who did it, they don't rewatch. The retention curve is
   front-loaded, which tanks the second-watch signal that powers the
   algorithm in week 2+.

Scam/fraud explainer content flips all three:

1. **Image-rich and clean.** A scam story is told through the *artifacts* —
   the text messages, the bank transfers, the profile photos, the dollar
   amounts. You can mock all of these in VOX style. You need maybe 3–5 real
   images per video (logos, a phone screenshot, a bank statement crop), the
   rest is generated or designed.

2. **Monetization king.** "Personal finance," "cybersecurity," and "how-to" all
   carry top-tier CPMs ($18–$35 RPM in the US). Scam explainers sit at the
   intersection. Adjacent advertisers: identity protection (Aura, Norton),
   antivirus (Bitdefender, Malwarebytes), banks (SoFi, Marcus), VPN
   (NordVPN, ExpressVPN), password managers (1Password). Every one of them
   actively buys scam-channel inventory.

3. **Evergreen + share-driven.** "How a romance scam cost a widow $400K" is
   shareable dinner-table content. People send it to their mom. The
   share-to-impression ratio is 3–4x true crime. You also get natural
   re-watches because viewers pause to read the screenshots.

### The niche validation

Working scam/fraud channels as of 2026 (study these, don't copy):

- **Coffeezilla** — ~5M subs. "Scam exposé" format. Long form, 15–25 min.
- **Jim Browning** — ~5M subs. "Scam-bait" — calls the scammers on video.
- **Pleasant Green** — ~2M subs. Investigation + interview format.
- **Scammer Payback** — ~10M subs. Live scam-baiting with hidden camera.
- **Vox's own "How Scams Work"** — ~3M views per episode, VOX style.

What's *missing* in the niche: **short-form, VOX-style scam explainers**
(60–90 second, paper aesthetic, hand-drawn annotations, kinetic text).
That's the white space. Coffeezilla is long-form. Vox is long-form. Nobody
owns the 60-second VOX explainer. **That's your lane.**

## PART 1 — THE 7 VIRAL SCAM ARCHETYPES

Pick one per episode. Each archetype has a built-in audience already searching
for it, so your title tags into existing demand.

### 1. The Pig Butchering Scam ("Sha Zhu Pan")

The big one. Originated in Chinese-organized crime rings, now global. Victim
is approached on a dating app or WhatsApp, groomed for weeks, then convinced
to "invest" in a fake crypto platform. The platform shows fake profits. The
victim tries to withdraw — they can't. By then, $50K–$1.5M is gone.

**Why it goes viral:** the mechanism is cinematic. The viewer watches a real
person get groomed in real time. Emotional gut-punch + financial horror.

**Vox angle:** "How $1.2 billion disappeared through a single fake app."

### 2. The Romance Scam

Long-form version of pig butchering but without the crypto angle. Older
victims. Emotional manipulation over months. The "ask" is emergency money:
medical bills, visa problems, a stuck shipment.

**Why it goes viral:** universal. Your mom, your aunt, your neighbor. People
forward it.

**Vox angle:** "The 6-month text conversation that cost one widow $400,000."

### 3. The CEO Wire Fraud (BEC)

Business Email Compromise. A finance clerk gets an email "from the CEO"
asking them to wire $250K to a vendor. The email is spoofed. The wire goes
out. It hits a Chinese or Hong Kong bank account and is gone in 4 hours.

**Why it goes viral:** corporate horror. CFO getting fired over a typo. The
average BEC loss in 2024 was $125K per incident. Total BEC losses: $2.9B
reported to the FBI in 2024 alone.

**Vox angle:** "How one email cost a 200-person company $25 million."

### 4. The Tech Support Scam

Microsoft/Apple impostor. Pop-up says "Your computer has a virus, call this
number." Victim calls. "Technician" takes remote access, shows fake
"infection," charges $500 for "fix" + a $5,000/year "protection plan."

**Why it goes viral:** happens to 60+ year-olds every day. The audience for
this video is people who want to send it to their parents.

**Vox angle:** "The fake virus that convinced grandpa to wire $4,800."

### 5. The Job Offer / Task Scam

WhatsApp/Telegram "recruiter" offers easy money: "Like 30 YouTube videos,
get $5." Then: "Pay $100 to unlock the next level." Then: "Pay $500 to
unlock withdrawals." Then: silence. Major 2024–2026 wave targeting South
Asia, Africa, and the US Filipino-American community.

**Why it goes viral:** the on-ramp looks legitimate. The viewer thinks "this
would have worked on me."

**Vox angle:** "The $5 task that turned into a $3,200 lesson."

### 6. The Crypto Rug Pull / Pump-and-Dump

A new coin launches. Influencers (often paid) tweet about it. Price goes 10x
in 24 hours. The founders drain the liquidity pool. Price goes to zero.
Investors lose everything.

**Why it goes viral:** numbers. "$50M disappeared in 11 minutes" is a
clickable headline.

**Vox angle:** "The $50M coin that survived exactly 11 minutes."

### 7. The Government / Tax Impersonator

IRS, DMV, Social Security, "your Medicare has been compromised." Caller ID
spoofed to look like a real government number. Victim told they owe back
taxes, must pay immediately via gift cards or wire transfer. Targets
first-generation immigrants and elderly in the US at massive scale.

**Why it goes viral:** the victim-blaming is real but the mechanism is wild.
Viewers learn something they didn't know was possible.

**Vox angle:** "The phone call that stole $18,000 in Target gift cards."

### Pacing the series

| Ep | Archetype | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Romance Scam | Universally relatable, low legal risk |
| 2 | Pig Butchering | Mechanism-led, VOX-perfect |
| 3 | CEO Wire Fraud | Corporate audience, high CPM |
| 4 | Tech Support Scam | Protects elderly parents — strong "send to family" signal |
| 5 | Job Offer Scam | Hooks the YouTube Shorts audience |
| 6 | Crypto Rug Pull | Crypto audience, high CTR |
| 7 | Government Impersonator | Authority angle, broad reach |

## PART 2 — THE NEW VISUAL LANGUAGE

The crime engine had two vocabularies: cinematic (dark, moody, archival) and
VOX (paper, ink, orange). Scam explainer keeps the VOX vocabulary and
*retires the cinematic one entirely*. Reason: scam stories aren't about
atmosphere, they're about artifacts. The mood is "calm explainer," not
"thriller."

### The Scam palette (add to `theme.ts`)

```ts
scam: {
  paper:    "#F5F1E8",  // warm off-white (slightly warmer than vox.paper)
  paperDeep:"#E8E1D2",  // edge fade
  ink:      "#1A1A1A",  // body text
  inkSoft:  "#3A3A38",  // secondary text
  rule:     "#C7BFAD",  // printer's guides
  accent:   "#D9491E",  // vox orange (for the callouts, the underlines, the "RED FLAG")
  alert:    "#C81E1E",  // alert red (for "THIS IS THE MOMENT IT GOES WRONG")
  money:    "#0E7C3A",  // money green (for amounts, the "$", the transfer arrows)
  verified: "#1B6FDB",  // verification blue (for the fake "verified" checkmarks)
  font:     "Archivo / Inter / Space Grotesk"  // same as vox
}
```

### Why these specific tokens

- `paper` and `paperDeep` — warm, not cold. Crime doc paper was #F4F1EA
  (slightly cool). Scam paper is warmer because the vibe is "sitting with
  a friend over coffee explaining how you almost got got," not "reading
  a true crime at midnight."
- `accent` (orange) — same Vox orange. This is your "look here" color. Every
  underline, every circle, every "RED FLAG" stamp uses it.
- `alert` (red) — NEW. Reserved for the exact moment the scam turns. The
  phone screen goes red. The transfer amount goes red. One beat per video
  max. Sparingly is what makes it hit.
- `money` (green) — for all dollar amounts. Money is the *thing* in scam
  content. Make it a color so the eye goes to it.
- `verified` (blue) — for the *fake* verification checkmarks that scammers
  use. Coloring these blue (the color of real trust signals) is the VOX
  meta-joke that makes scam content feel smart.

### The 5 visual types (replace crime's 13 modules)

Crime had 13 modules. Scam needs 5. Less is more.

| Module | What it does | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| `chat` | Renders a phone chat mockup (text bubbles, profile pic, time stamps) | Every "this is what they said" beat — 40% of beats |
| `transfer` | Renders a bank/wire transfer screen (amount, from/to, status) | Every "this is when the money moved" beat — 20% |
| `annotation` | Hand-drawn roughjs circle/underline/arrow over a phone/transfer image | The "look at this" beats — 20% |
| `kinetic` | Big bold text slamming in word-by-word | The hook, the reveal, the lesson — 15% |
| `chart` | Ink line drawing in (timeline of money flow, $ over time) | The "this is the scale" beats — 5% |

That's it. 5 modules, each one you already have the *bones* for in
`vox/scenes.tsx`. The work is `chat` and `transfer` — these are the new
pieces, and they're mockup generators, not code. The next part covers
that.

## PART 3 — THE FEW REAL IMAGES STRATEGY

You said "I'll need only a few real images." You're right. The full
10-minute VOX explainer needs:

| Type | Count | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform logos (WhatsApp, Instagram, Chase Bank) | 8–12 | SVG, official brand kits | $0 |
| Phone chat screenshots (anonymized) | 0–2 | Design in Figma/Canva, OR find published in news articles | $0 |
| Bank statement mockups | 0–1 | Design in Figma | $0 |
| Dollar bill / stack of cash | 0 | Generate via Pollinations (no real money photography) | $0 |
| Profile photo of scammer | 0 | Use Vox icon (user-x) or silhouette — NEVER a real face | $0 |
| Map (where the money flowed) | 1 | Designed SVG, no AI needed | $0 |
| News headline clipping | 0–1 | Generate via VOX `quote` module (no real newspaper) | $0 |
| Generic B-roll (hands on keyboard, person on phone) | 3–5 | Pexels (free API, you have the tool) | $0 |

**Total real-image load: 5–10 images per video.** Everything else is
generated via Pollinations (free, unlimited) or designed in code (the
chat/transfer mockups).

### The "no criminal images" advantage

This is the big unlock. Crime doc needed 100+ real images (or the
equivalent AI-generated images that you were worried about). Scam explainer
needs maybe 5. The rest is type, charts, and mockup UIs — all of which
are *more* engaging than photographs in this niche because they
*teach*. A photo of a phone is wallpaper. A mockup of the actual scam
text message is content.

## PART 4 — STORY FORMAT (UPDATED)

Same beat block structure as the crime engine, but with three new fields
and the `module` defaults simplified.

### Beat block — new fields

```markdown
### BEAT 4 — THE HOOK (0:15–0:25)

| layer | content |
|---|---|
| **vo** | "It started with a single message." |
| **text** | IT STARTED WITH A MESSAGE |
| **chat** | whatsapp: Catherine: Hi! I saw your profile. How are you? · profile: anonymous silhouette · accent: orange |
| **icon** | message-circle: WHATSAPP, heart: ROMANCE, gift: GIFT |
| **doodle** | underline under "Catherine" |
| **sfx** | notification ping |
```

Three new things:

- **`chat:`** — instead of `image_prompt`. Tells the chat module what to
  render. Format: `platform: speaker: message · speaker: message`. The
  module handles layout.
- **`transfer:`** — same idea but for the bank UI mockup.
  Format: `from: account · to: account · amount: $50,000 · status: pending`.
- **`alert:`** — optional. If present, that beat fires the alert red palette
  and a small "⚠ RED FLAG" kicker in the corner. One beat per video.

### Module defaults (simplified)

The parser auto-picks the module based on which row is filled:

| Row present | Module picked |
|---|---|
| `chat` | `chat` |
| `transfer` | `transfer` |
| `chart_data` | `chart` |
| `icon` | `icon` |
| `doodle` | `annotation` |
| none of the above | `kinetic` |

That removes the "what module should I use?" decision from the writer. You
fill the rows, the engine picks the visual.

## PART 5 — NEW TOOLS TO BUILD

Three small tools. The first two are image generators that *are not AI* —
they are code that builds a UI mockup. The third is the existing
Pollinations fetcher, repurposed.

### Tool 1: `tools/chat-mockup.py`

Reads a `chat:` row from a beat, generates a PNG of a phone chat screen
with the right platform color, profile silhouette, message bubbles, and
timestamps. Output: `public/footage/chat-N.png`. Resolution: 1080×1920
(we'll downscale inside Remotion).

Library: **Pillow** + **CairoSVG** (already in Python's standard toolkit
for most ops). No AI involved. Render time: ~0.3s per chat. Pure design.

```python
# skeleton — full impl is ~150 lines
def render_chat(beat: dict, dest: Path) -> None:
    """Beat with a 'chat' field -> png of a phone chat screen."""
    spec = parse_chat_field(beat["chat"])
    img = Image.new("RGB", (1080, 1920), (245, 241, 232))  # paper
    draw = ImageDraw.Draw(img)
    # header bar (platform color)
    draw.rectangle([0, 0, 1080, 180], fill=PLATFORM_COLORS[spec.platform])
    # profile silhouette (centered icon, no face)
    draw.ellipse([460, 220, 620, 380], fill=(180, 180, 180))
    # message bubbles
    y = 460
    for msg in spec.messages:
        # ... bubble drawing logic, alternating left/right
        y += bubble_height + 24
    img.save(dest)
```

### Tool 2: `tools/transfer-mockup.py`

Same pattern but for the bank transfer screen. Reads a `transfer:` row.
Output: `public/footage/transfer-N.png`. Mock bank UI with the amount
prominent, "from" and "to" account numbers (truncated), status pill.

### Tool 3: `tools/fetch-vox-images.py`

Your existing pollinations fetcher, unchanged. The `image_prompt:` row
still works for the few beats that need a generated photograph (like
"stack of cash" or "generic phone on a desk"). Most beats won't use it
anymore — the `chat:` and `transfer:` rows cover the visual need.

### Tool 4: `tools/parse-story.mjs` (update)

Three changes:

1. Read the new rows: `chat`, `transfer`, `alert`.
2. Module picker uses the table from Part 4.
3. If a beat has `alert: true`, mark it `beat.alert = true` in
   `script.json` so the `theme.scam.alert` color fires on that beat only.

### Tool 5: `video/src/scam/elements.tsx` (new)

The VOX kit renamed. Copy `video/src/vox/elements.tsx`, change `vox` to
`scam` everywhere, add the `AlertKicker` and `MoneyAmount` primitives
that the new modules need.

### Tool 6: `video/src/scam/scenes.tsx` (new)

Five modules, each one already partially built in `vox/scenes.tsx`:

- `ChatScene` — calls into `chat-mockup.py`'s output, overlays annotation
  doodle if present.
- `TransferScene` — same.
- `AnnotationScene` — current `vox/doodle` module, copy + theme swap.
- `KineticScene` — current `vox/kinetic` module, copy + theme swap.
- `ChartScene` — current `vox/chart` module, copy + theme swap.

The `crime/` directory stays in the tree (don't delete it — your old
render still uses it) but no new crime content is added. `scam/` is
the active engine.

### Tool 7: `video/src/scam/ScamShort.tsx` (new)

The `VoxShort.tsx` clone, but swapped:

- `theme.vox` → `theme.scam`
- `VOX_MODULES` → `SCAM_MODULES`
- Same staging (PaperBG, camera, duck, music, SFX) — those work as-is

The vertical cut for Shorts. The horizontal cut for long-form is the
same `ScamShort.tsx` registered twice in `Root.tsx` with different
dimensions (per the pattern in your current `CrimeDoc.tsx`).

End-to-end. The first time you run this is the slowest (~3 hours, mostly


## PART 9 — VIRAL STRATEGY (THE ALGORITHM)

This is the part that makes the difference between 1K views and 1M.

### The niche signal hygiene

YouTube's classifier reads your channel as the *aggregate* of your
last 30 uploads. To be classified as "scam/fraud explainer":

1. **Channel name** — rename to something on-niche. Options:
   - "Ungot" (rhymes with "un-phished", short, memorable)
   - "After the Ask" (the moment a scam reveals itself)
   - "Receipts" (the proof-of-trick aesthetic)
   - "@ungot" / "@aftertheask" / "@receipts" — pick first available

2. **Description** — first two lines are everything:
   ```
   How scams actually work. One mechanism, 60 seconds, the receipts.

   Every scam has a turning point — the moment a person decides to
   send the money. We show you that moment, the conversation that
   led to it, and the one red flag nobody caught.
   ```

3. **Tags per video** — `scam`, `how scams work`, `romance scam`,
   `pig butchering`, `fraud explained`, `consumer protection`,
   `online scam`, `wire fraud`, `crypto scam`. Skip "true crime" entirely
   (it pulls the wrong audience).

### The first-30-seconds lock

This is the same rule from the crime plan. 70% of your distribution
hinges on the first 30 seconds. The structure:

- **sec 0–1**: hard cut. Black. One white word: "WIDOWED."
- **sec 1–3**: "$400,000."  (kinetic, money green)
- **sec 3–6**: "She sent it to a man she had never met." (kinetic, split
  into 3 word-slams)
- **sec 6–9**: "It started with a single message." (chat screen, 1 bubble)
- **sec 9–12**: the groom. (chat stack, 4–6 bubbles)
- **sec 12–30**: lead-in to beat 1 of the script.

**Rule: the full hook is legible on frame one.** No "intro music plays
while title fades in." No "subscribe for more." Hard cuts only until
minute 8.

### The share trigger

Scam content's viral mechanism is *defensive sharing* — people send
it to vulnerable family members. Two moves make this stronger:

1. **End the video with a "send this to" CTA** at minute 8+, after
   you've delivered value. One line: "If your mom is on Facebook, send
   her this video."

2. **Make the thumbnails look like warnings**, not entertainment.
   Yellow background, black text, the red alert color on the number.
   Look: a fake "RED FLAG" stamp over a phone screen with a money
   amount. That's the thumbnail grammar. Viewer sees it, thinks "oh
   god, my aunt would fall for that," clicks.

### The 9:16 → 16:9 → 9:16 compounding

| Day | Asset | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | 75-second Short (9:16) | YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels |
| Day 7 | 4–6 minute long-form (16:9) | YouTube main feed, embed in description of the Short |
| Day 14 | 30-second follow-up Short (9:16) | "The follow-up to last week's $400K scam — here's the part 2..." |
| Day 21 | Next episode (75s Short) | New scam, new hook, new comment section |

The Short pulls viewers from the feed → the long-form captures them →
the next Short rides the subscriber base. Compounding.

### The comment section playbook

Reply to *every* comment in the first 4 hours. Especially the "this
happened to my mom" comments. The replies themselves become content:
"Thank you for sharing. If anyone here has been affected, the FTC
accepts reports at reportfraud.ftc.gov." Free, authoritative, builds
trust with the algo and the audience.

## PART 13 — THE TRIPWIRES

Set these as explicit checks. They prevent you from spending 6 months
on a thing that isn't working.

### Tripwire 1: Day 21, "is the niche signal sticking?"

- Channel name, description, banner, all updated
- 3 Shorts shipped, 0 crime content visible (all privated)
- Open YouTube Studio → Channel → Analytics → "Top audience segments"
- **You should see "Personal Finance" or "News & Politics > Consumer
  Protection" in the top 3 segments within 21 days.**
- If not, the channel rebrand didn't take. Repeat Steps in Part 9 §1
  with different naming. Do not blame the videos.

### Tripwire 2: Day 30, "is the hook working?"

- 4 Shorts shipped, each with different opening
- Open YouTube Studio → each Short → "Viewed vs. Swiped Away"
- **If any Short is below 50% VVSA, the hook failed. Rewrite the
  first 3 seconds and re-upload as a new video.**
- If 3 of 4 Shorts are below 50%, the topic isn't the problem — the
  hook *grammar* is. Go rewatch the top 5 scam channels' first 3
  seconds of every video. Note the pattern. Mimic it.

### Tripwire 3: Day 60, "is the long form working?"

- 4 Shorts + 1 long-form shipped
- Long form should have: avg view duration > 1:30 (25% retention on a
  6-min video), CTR > 5%
- **If avg view duration < 0:45, the long form is too long. Cut to
  4 min. If still < 0:45, cut to 90 seconds. Some channels ONLY work
  as Shorts. That's fine.**
- The Short → long form funnel is the asymmetric bet. Shorts grow
  the channel; long form monetizes. If long form doesn't monetize,
  go 100% Shorts.

### Tripwire 4: Day 90, "is this a business?"

- 12+ Shorts shipped
- 1K+ subs
- 10K+ total views
- Any sponsorship inquiry (even declined)?
- **If no to all four, the niche isn't taking. Consider: (a) tighter
  hook, (b) different scam archetype, (c) different platform (TikTok
  may be more forgiving for new scam channels than YouTube in 2026).**
- Do not "try harder on the same thing." Pivot.
